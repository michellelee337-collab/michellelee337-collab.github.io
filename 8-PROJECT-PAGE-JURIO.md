## The problem

Korean labor law protects workers who often cannot read it. A worker facing unpaid wages, a sudden contract non-renewal, or an injury on the job has to translate their situation into legal vocabulary — 체불, 산재, 부당해고 — before they can even search for help. For migrant workers navigating this in a second or third language, the distance between "something is wrong" and "I know what this is called and who to ask" can decide whether they get protection at all.

## What Jurio does

Jurio is a bilingual (Korean/English) legal-information app that starts from the worker's situation, not the statute:

- **Plain-language issue guides** — entries like "I didn't get paid" (임금을 못 받았어요), "I was hurt at work" (일하다 다쳤어요), and contract expiration walk users from a real situation toward the relevant legal concepts.
- **Statute cards with real citations** — key provisions of the Labor Standards Act (e.g., restrictions on dismissal, written notice of grounds, wage-claim limitation periods), the Minimum Wage Act, and the Foreign Workers Employment Act (workplace-change rules), each linked to the official statute database (law.go.kr).
- **A glossary of labor-law terminology** explained in plain language.
- **A directory of certified labor attorneys (공인노무사)** and labor-law offices, organized by region — connecting information to actual professional help.
- **Resource navigation for migrant workers** — multilingual hotlines including Danuri (1577-1366, 13 languages), the foreign-workforce counseling line (1644-0644, 18 languages), and the 1345 immigration information center.
- **Practical documentation guidance** — e.g., wage-arrears confirmation documents (체불 확인서).
