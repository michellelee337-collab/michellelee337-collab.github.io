## Overview

Angels for the Journey is a youth-driven network that helps children in the Philippines, Cambodia, and Laos stay in school. Since 2021, it has delivered 187 individual supports — tuition, uniforms, exam fees, school supplies, and emergency family aid — working through local coordinators who identify needs and confirm every delivery. The organizing idea is in the name: not rescue, but accompaniment — staying with the same children and families, step by step, year by year.

## Origin

The project began with a question about how charity usually works. Visiting homes, churches, and schools in the rural Cavite region of the Philippines while reporting for a youth newspaper, the founding team kept seeing the same pattern: a group would arrive once a year, hand out gifts, take photos, and leave. Angels for the Journey was built as the opposite of that pattern — a network of young volunteers committed to the unglamorous middle of a child's education: the school fees that accumulate, the uniform that wears out, the family crisis that would otherwise end a school year.

## What the project does

- **Keeps children in school** — tuition, uniforms, exam fees, and supplies, with needs identified by local coordinators and every delivery documented and confirmed with families.
- **Steadies families in crisis** — short-term, specific aid (food staples, hygiene essentials) for particular families at particular moments, clearly documented rather than open-ended.
- **Opens doors after graduation** — U.S. college application guidance for older students, including scholarship pathways explored with partners such as CETUSA.
- **Builds youth-to-youth connection** — free Zoom academic support (including 1:1 sessions) and cultural-exchange workshops connecting volunteers and students across countries; letters and language exchange in between.

## Outcomes

As of July 2026, the network has delivered **187 student supports** across three countries. Its longest-running relationships now reach past high school: in December 2025, **Chanra Hun** of Kok Trach Village, Siem Reap — supported through her secondary education — was admitted to **Reed College** (Portland, Oregon, Class of 2030) with the college's Continental Scholarship, and plans to study psychology and mathematics. *(Confirm Chanra's consent to be named/shown; her story is already public on the organization's site.)*

## How the work stays honest

The organization's practices are deliberately unromantic:
- Local coordinators — not visiting volunteers — identify who needs what.
- Every support is documented and photo-confirmed with the family.
- Volunteer field visits are structured around joint activities with local schools, with evening reflection circles and post-trip debriefs on privilege and inequality — volunteers are asked to learn, not just to give.

## What this project shows about Michelle

Five years of staying. The project's value as evidence is not its size but its duration and its discipline: a commitment made at 2021 prices and still being paid in 2026, with paperwork to show for it. It is also where Michelle's later work begins — the tutoring became curriculum, the letters became translation, and the question "what keeps a child out of school?" became a question about systems, which is now taking her into law.
