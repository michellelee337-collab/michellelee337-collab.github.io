## Overview

Lumen Education Program is a publication-centered social venture based in Phnom Penh, co-founded by Minjung (Michelle) Lee with Alex Lee. Working with Cambodian youth and educators, Lumen adapts local folktales and everyday experiences into illustrated children's books in Khmer, English, and Korean, then places those books in libraries and classrooms in communities where age-appropriate books in a child's own language are hard to find. Proceeds from book sales are reinvested in further donations and new reading spaces.

## Why it started

In Phnom Penh and Siem Reap, the contrast is hard to miss: tourists, hotels, and souvenir shops on one side; children with almost no access to age-appropriate books in their own language on the other. After consulting teachers, parents, and young people, Michelle and her co-founder built Lumen as a small, practical bridge — a Cambodian publication turning local oral stories into books children can actually hold.

## The approach

Four commitments shape the work:
1. **Well-made and culturally familiar** — children deserve books where the settings, foods, and families look like their own.
2. **Adaptation with permission** — folktales are retold with context and input from the people who live them.
3. **Youth as co-authors** — Cambodian young people are credited co-creators, not sources; stories have been developed with co-authors including Mey Yean, Kun Thea, Villay, Sreynoch, Thaily, and Rota.
4. **Revenue returns to readers** — sales fund further donations and library creation.

## Publications

- ***Amok*** (Khmer · English · Korean) — Srey Leak learns to cook fish amok with her grandmother during Pchum Ben: a story about how a single dish carries family memory, gratitude, and Khmer culture.
- ***Phnom Pros and Phnom Srei*** (Khmer · English · Korean) — a retelling of the Kampong Cham legend of Men's Hill and Women's Hill, where wisdom outbuilds strength.
- Additional original stories in development with youth co-authors. *(Confirm current list.)*

## Libraries & reading spaces

Lumen's books helped create **three children's libraries in Siem Reap** (with Angels for the Journey), where children can browse freely and read alone, with friends, or with teachers — practicing reading in both Khmer and English. In Phnom Penh, Lumen donated **205 books to the National Library of Cambodia**, acknowledged in a letter of gratitude from the Library's director.

## Workshops & partnerships

With partners including the National Library of Cambodia, Royal University of Phnom Penh, Sipar, the Women's Resource Center, Hesed School, and Tent of David sites, Lumen runs book workshops — read-alouds, vocabulary games, children drawing scenes or retelling tales in their own variations — and free **Korean language and cultural-exchange Zoom classes** for Cambodian youth, librarians, and adult learners.
