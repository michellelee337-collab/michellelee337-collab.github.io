# Michelle Lee — Website Redesign Master Plan

Single authoritative build plan. Supersedes the scattered strategy notes (1, 2, 6, 9, 10, 11) where they conflict. Written to fix every weakness in the critical assessment. Nothing here invents a number, date, award, or outcome; anything unverifiable is marked **[Needs confirmation]**.

---

## 1. Executive diagnosis

**Biggest problem:** the site is ~70% built but 100% visible. The architecture, the one-story spine, and the writing are strong; the site is held back by **incompleteness that shows** — visible `[placeholder]` boxes, a dead Contact button (`mailto:[confirm-public-email]`), and 10 of 13 homepage images rendered as cropped typographic tiles instead of photographs.

**Is it design, structure, storytelling, content, visuals, or credibility?** Not design (the system is elegant), not storytelling (the arc is coherent). It is **content-completeness and visuals**, and those two are dragging **credibility** down with them. A reviewer reads visible placeholders as carelessness — fatal for someone presenting as a writer.

**Most important single fix:** purge every placeholder from public view and replace the 10 tiles with real photographs already sitting in the source sites. That one move takes the site from "promising draft" to "publishable."

**What it should become:** a warm, editorial, image-first personal site where a busy reader understands in 10 seconds that Michelle is a writer-educator-translator working across borders, sees her single best proof (the published essay) immediately, and can reach her strongest work in one click — without the site ever announcing what it is "for."

---

## 2. Final website strategy (one paragraph)

The site is for a **busy, serious reader** — a mentor, professor, editor, fellowship reviewer, or counselor who has two to five minutes. It should leave the impression of a **mature, globally-minded writer and educator whose community work and research share one intellectual spine, not a busy student with a long list.** The story it tells: *language and storytelling create access and dignity — Michelle has spent years carrying meaning across languages, classrooms, borders, and now legal systems.* After two minutes the visitor should remember **one image (a Cambodian classroom or library), one credential (a published, prize-honored essay), and one phrase ("access and dignity across borders").** The next thing they should click is **"A Pagoda"** (proof of writing) or **one project** (proof of building).

## 3. Core narrative

> Minjung (Michelle) Lee is a writer, educator, and translator who moves stories across languages, borders, and systems — so that overlooked communities can be understood and served.

**Three taglines (pick one; concrete beats poetic for a busy reader):**
1. *Writer, educator, and translator working across Korea, Cambodia, and the U.S.*
2. *Stories across languages, borders, and systems.*

**Three pillars:**
- **Language as access** — translation, interpretation, multilingual publishing, plain-language legal information.
- **Storytelling as dignity** — journalism, children's books, essays; people as authors, not subjects.
- **Education as public service** — teaching, literacy projects, long-term youth support.

**Short About message (≤55 words):**
> I grew up between New Zealand, the U.S., and Korea, and I've spent the years since carrying meaning across borders — translating for migrant families, teaching writing in Cambodia, reporting on memory and migration, and building projects where language, education, and law meet.

**Homepage message (one line under the name):**

**How all projects connect (one sentence):**
